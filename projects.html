Featured project – TIPE: passive autorotation for drone parcel drops

Preparatory classes – PSI Team of 2 Theme: "Loops and Cycles"

The TIPE (Travail d'Initiative Personnelle Encadré) is a supervised research project presented at the French engineering school entrance exams. With my teammate, we studied how a freely rotating propeller can slow down a parcel dropped from a drone, without any motor or parachute: the airflow makes the blades spin (autorotation), which generates lift and brakes the fall – the same principle as a sycamore seed or a helicopter after engine failure.

Approach

  • Modelling: we derived a non-linear ordinary differential equation for the vertical motion and obtained an analytical solution in tanh, which gives the terminal velocity.
  • Simulation: the analytical solution was cross-checked with a fourth-order Runge–Kutta (RK4) solver written in Python.
  • Design & manufacturing: we designed a propeller with a Clark Y airfoil in OnShape, using FeatureScript to generate the blade geometry, then 3D-printed it.
  • Experiments: drops from a height of 12 m, filmed at 240 fps; trajectories were extracted frame by frame with the Tracker video-analysis software.

What I learned

  • Building a physical model, simplifying it wisely, and validating it numerically.
  • Going through the whole engineering loop: theory → CAD → prototype → measurement → critical analysis.
  • Explaining the gap between model and reality (blade losses, Reynolds number effects, printing tolerances…).
